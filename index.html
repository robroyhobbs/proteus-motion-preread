What stood out to me

Proteus feels like a business where the raw material for strong growth already exists

1. A real category-level product

Proteus is not selling generic fitness equipment. It is selling a new way to measure and train human movement: 3D resistance, measurable power/strength/speed, and data that means something in the real world.

That matters because the strongest growth stories are not built from slogans. They are built from a sharp point of view plus proof that the point of view is true.

2. Strong proof from credible users

From the outside, Proteus already has strong social proof: respected trainers, high-performance use cases, and a product story that sounds easier to believe once buyers can see how it changes training, rehab, or assessment in practice.

That is the kind of company where better packaging and better distribution can create disproportionate leverage.

The opportunity

This is not simply “do more marketing”

The core opportunity is to turn Proteus’s existing product differentiation, customer proof, and founder conviction into a more systematic demand engine.

What that likely means in practice

  • Sharpening the message by audience instead of talking to everyone the same way
  • Packaging proof so it travels well across channels
  • Turning strong long-form source material into many platform-native assets
  • Making the founder point of view easier to discover and trust
  • Creating a cleaner bridge from attention to conversation to sale

My bias

I would rather help produce fewer, sharper assets that make trainers, clinicians, and performance-minded buyers think “these people actually understand the problem” than chase generic reach.

For a company like Proteus, credibility matters more than volume.

How I’d think about Proteus specifically

Four practical areas I’d focus on first

1. Segment the story by wedge

Proteus appears to serve multiple valuable audiences — elite performance, rehab / return to play, coaches and trainers, and facilities that need a differentiated offering.

Those buyers may all love the same machine, but they do not buy for the same reason. I would want each wedge to have its own message, proof stack, and funnel.

2. Build a usable proof library

Proteus seems to have unusually good proof assets available: trainer validation, athlete use cases, measurable outputs, and a strong founder-origin story.

I would want to turn that into a system: flagship case studies, short proof snippets for sales and social, vertical pages, founder posts, and before/after stories tied to outcomes.

3. Get more leverage from every strong source

A single case study, coach interview, customer Q&A, or founder conversation should not produce one blog post. It should produce a sequence: long-form story, LinkedIn posts, X threads, short-form video angles, sales collateral, email content, and website proof sections.

4. Keep the voice intelligent and grounded

For a company like Proteus, the job is not to sound louder. It is to sound more credible, more insightful, and more obviously connected to the actual problem the buyer is trying to solve.

Discussion wedges

Three paths I’d want to explore in conversation

Performance / elite training

  • Which teams, trainers, or athlete profiles are the strongest fit today?
  • What do your best customers believe about Proteus that prospects still miss?
  • Which proof changes a skeptical coach’s mind fastest?

Rehab / return to play

  • How are PT / rehab buyers evaluating Proteus right now?
  • Which measurement or outcome stories matter most in that segment?
  • What practitioner stories deserve more visibility?

Facilities / operators

  • How do facilities justify the purchase internally?
  • Is the message more about outcomes, differentiation, retention, or pricing power?
  • What does the strongest sales conversation look like today?
